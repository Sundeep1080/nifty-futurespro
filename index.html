Why Greeks / IV are model estimates, not exchange-quoted values
NSE's live feed only ever carries five fields per contract: LTP, bid/ask, volume, OI, day OHLC. It never carries Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega or IV — no exchange anywhere publishes those, because a Greek isn't a traded price, it's a partial derivative of a pricing model. There's no model-free "true" Delta sitting in the market; it only exists once you fix a model, a volatility input, and a rate input. That's also why two platforms can legitimately disagree on the same contract at the same second:
  • Forward vs spot — this page prices NIFTY options off the futures price as the forward (Black-76), because the futures price already embeds the market's real cost-of-carry/dividend expectation. Pricing off raw spot with a guessed dividend yield (what most simple tools do) introduces a small, avoidable bias.
  • Which price you solve IV from — this page uses the live bid/ask midpoint when available, falling back to LTP only if depth is empty. A stale LTP in a thin strike gives you a stale IV.
  • Solver robustness — IV is found numerically (Newton-Raphson), which can fail to converge on deep ITM/OTM strikes near expiry. This page falls back to bisection so a strike doesn't just silently show "—".
  • Rate assumption — the risk-free rate above is a manual input, same as every retail platform; none of them are using your exact funding rate either.
Net effect: these Greeks are a reasonable, methodologically sound estimate — not a display bug, and not something any vendor (Spider included) can make "exchange-accurate," because that phrase doesn't apply to a modeled quantity.
